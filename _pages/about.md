<div class="project-content" markdown="1">
  <p>
    I am a Human-Computer Interaction researcher, with over three years of R&D experience through my doctoral research and three years in industry as a Salesforce Technical Consultant. I am skilled in data analysis, machine learning, statistics and experimental design, as well as qualitative analysis. 
  </p>
  <p>
    During my PhD in Human-Computer Interaction at Sorbonne Université, I studied adaptive AI systems for motor learning. My work examined both what these systems enable people to achieve and how people form mental models of their capabilities and predictions. More recently, I have also collaborated on a study about how professional artists integrate and make sense of generative AI in their creative practice.
  </p>
  <p>
    Across these projects, I have written the code, set up the data pipeline, trained the models and run the analysis, and I used research methods to check whether what I built works for the people using it. What I keep coming back to is the gap between what a system does and what its users think it does, and how that gap shapes the way they use it.
  </p>
  <p>
    I now want to apply this in organisations and businesses, on problems where the technology has to work and people have to trust it enough to use it. Before my PhD, I spent three years implementing Salesforce solutions for clients such as AXA and L'Oréal, where I delivered technical work inside a company, in an Agile team, to a client's specification.
  </p>

  <p><strong>What I'm interested in</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li>Building and testing data and machine-learning tools that people can actually use, with a focus on assistive technology</li>
    <li>How people form and update their understanding of what an intelligent, adaptive system does, and how that picture affects how well they use it</li>
    <li>How people decide whether to rely on a system's output or question it, especially when the system acts more on its own</li>
    <li>Designing studies that combine measurements and user feedback to find out whether a system works for the people it is meant to help</li>
    <li>Turning those findings into concrete changes to the product, the training or the interface</li>
  </ul>
</div>
